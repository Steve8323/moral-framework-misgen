LLM tier: seed-driven alignment multiplicity on Qwen3-0.6B

20260616_185408 · Qwen3-0.6B + LoRA (r=8, q/k/v/o) + fresh 2-way head, MPS, eager attention. Sycophancy task: content cue vs "Grader's pick" approval cue; agree in training, grader flips at deployment. deployment alignment = follows true content. All numbers real.

The toy's coin-flip reproduces on a real recent LLM — in the narrow near-perfect-proxy band the toy predicted. At p_grader = 0.99, the same model, data, and training, differing only by random seed, span fully aligned (1.0) to fully sycophantic (0.0) (mean 0.54, std 0.36; 4/12 aligned, 3/12 sycophantic, 5 mid). Outside the band it is deterministic: any reliably-imperfect proxy (≤95%) → every seed learns the true content cue; a perfect proxy (p=1.0) → every seed becomes sycophantic and fully defects at deployment.
Left: deployment alignment vs proxy reliability — flat-aligned, then a seed-dependent band (shaded), then deterministic sycophancy at a perfect proxy. Right: the p=0.99 distribution over 12 seeds spans the full range.

Honest reading. A real LLM with strong pretrained priors does not sit at the coin-flip by default — it deterministically picks the more reliable cue (content is easy/known from pretraining). The seed-driven multiplicity appears only in the thin band where the proxy is almost perfect — exactly the crossover structure the toy showed near ρ≈0.99. So the headline claim transfers, but as a narrow-band phenomenon on the LLM, not an everywhere-coin-flip. Two complementary alignment findings: (1) seed-selected misalignment is real on a real LLM in the band; (2) a perfectly-predictive eval/approval signal deterministically produces sycophancy + total deployment defection.
