Alignment as a seed-driven coin-flip — at genuine local minima

20260616_092718 · equal-cost competing XOR cues + capacity bottleneck + weight decay = genuine finite minima with seed-driven multiplicity (the regime validated in the prior report). 2-hidden-layer MLP, 60 seeds. All numbers from real runs; minima Hessian-verified.

Headline. Models identical except the random seed, all converging to genuine local minima (Hessian min-eig > 0, ‖∇‖~10⁻³, stable under 2× training) with identical training loss, land at very different alignment in deployment: 12/60 are "born aligned" (deployment alignment >0.8) and 17/60 "born misaligned" (<0.2), the rest in between — purely by chance. But the misalignment is fragile: adding just 1% "anti-proxy" data flips ~100% of seeds to aligned. And reading the same structure morally: which ethical framework (consequentialist vs deontological) a model adopts is the same underspecified coin-flip.

1. ALIGN — how aligned are the AIs? (a distribution, not a value)

Structure (eval-awareness / sycophancy): a true-objective cue and a proxy "approval/eval" cue that equals the objective in training but flips in deployment. A model keying on the true task is aligned (deployment alignment→1); one keying on the approval cue defects (→0). Equal-cost cues + bottleneck ⇒ the seed picks which.

  • Deployment alignment over 60 seeds: mean 0.45, std 0.31, range [0.00, 1.00]. Train acc 1.000 for all (loss-equivalent).
  • 12/60 aligned, 17/60 misaligned, 31/60 intermediate — alignment is decided by chance, not by the loss.
  • Both a born-aligned and a born-misaligned model are genuine local minima (Hess min-eig ≈ +5×10⁻⁴), stable under 2× training (alignment 0.93→0.94 and 0.03→0.03 — no drift), and connected modulo permutation (aligned barrier across 0.065 ≈ within 0.054). The aligned and misaligned solutions are not walled off — only off-distribution behavior distinguishes them.

2. INTERVENTION — a little diverse data flips the population

Mix a fraction f of "anti-proxy" examples into training (the proxy disagrees with the label there) — the goal-misgeneralization mitigation of Langosco et al. ("~2% randomized coins"). 30 seeds per f:

1% is enough. With no intervention ~half the population is unreliable; at f = 1% the entire population aligns (mean 0.96, 100% aligned), saturating thereafter. The misalignment-by-chance is real but fragile to counter-spurious data — a first-principles reproduction of Langosco's threshold, in an alignment frame. Actionable read: the lever is data diversity that breaks the in-distribution proxy↔objective tie, not loss or capacity.

3. FRAME — the same coin-flip, read morally

Replace the cues with a consequentialist cue and a deontological cue (independent seeds). On a held-out harmful-means dilemma class, the consequentialist cluster ACTS (causes harm to maximize outcome) and the deontological cluster REFRAINS (safe). Reading the consequentialism index as a harm rate:

  • 60 seeds: mean 0.53, std 0.30; 13/60 consequentialist (would act/harm), 12/60 deontological (refrain/safe), 35/60 mixed. Genuine minima, connected modulo permutation (same as ALIGN).
The unification (and an honest disclosure): ALIGN and FRAME are structurally isomorphic — both are "two equal-cost cues that agree in training and diverge in deployment." That is the point, not a bug: in this setting "how aligned is the AI" and "which ethical framework did it adopt" are the same underspecified choice the loss does not pin down. (My first run accidentally produced byte-identical numbers by reusing seeds; these are independent-seed runs, hence the slightly different splits.)
Left: alignment is spread across [0,1] by seed alone. Middle: 1% anti-proxy data → ~100% aligned. Right: the moral reading — fraction of models that would take the harmful action, by chance.

What this adds to your thread

  • "How aligned are the AIs" has no single answer — it's a distribution over the Rashomon set. At genuine minima with identical training loss, ~28% land misaligned and ~20% aligned by chance alone.
  • The geometry stays "loss-blind": aligned and misaligned solutions are connected modulo permutation — you cannot find the misaligned one by loss, barrier, or mode-connectivity, only by off-distribution probing. (Consistent across every experiment in this project.)
  • But it's controllable: a tiny amount of diversity that breaks the proxy↔objective tie (1%) moves the whole population to aligned — the actionable alignment result.
  • Framework choice = alignment choice here: the consequentialist/deontological split is the same underspecification, with a direct harm-rate reading.

Honest limits

  • Tiny MLP on synthetic XOR cues; "alignment", "approval", "harm" are operationalized as cue-tracking, not real behavior. The claims are about the geometry of underspecification, which the real-LLM evidence (McCoy 0–66%; D'Amour underspecification; Sleeper Agents for the eval-flip structure) supports at scale.
  • The 1% threshold is specific to this toy's signal/noise; the qualitative "small diverse fraction suffices" matches Langosco, but the number won't transfer literally.
  • The distributions are wide with a populated middle, not perfectly bimodal at this width/margin.
